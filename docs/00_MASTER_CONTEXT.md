# 1. Project Overview

## Project Name

Discora

## One-Line Description

Discora is a structured discourse platform that transforms chaotic online discussions into organized, evidence-based conversations and debates.

## Project Summary

Discora is designed to improve the quality of online discourse by providing a structured environment where users can discuss ideas, share perspectives, examine evidence, challenge assumptions, and reach more informed conclusions.

Unlike traditional social media platforms that prioritize engagement, popularity, and reactions, Discora prioritizes understanding, transparency, evidence, and critical thinking.

Discussions and debates on Discora are organized around claims, evidence, sources, questions, consensus, and open questions. The platform is designed to help participants and observers understand not only what people believe, but why they believe it.

Discora is not intended to determine winners and losers. Instead, it aims to help individuals explore complex topics, understand opposing viewpoints, identify common ground, and build more informed perspectives.

---

# 2. Vision Statement

Discora is a structured discourse platform built to improve the quality of online discussions and debates. In a world where conversations are often driven by emotion, misinformation, personal bias, and fragmented opinions, Discora provides a space where ideas can be explored through evidence, reasoning, and open discussion.

By organizing claims, sources, counterarguments, and perspectives in a clear and transparent way, Discora helps people move beyond noise, assumptions, and echo chambers. Discussions can evolve into structured debates, evidence can be examined in context, and agreements, disagreements, and unanswered questions can be clearly understood.

Discora does not tell people what to think. Instead, it empowers individuals to challenge ideas, understand different viewpoints, and form their own informed conclusions while preserving freedom of thought, expression, and respectful disagreement.

---

# 3. Mission Statement

To make meaningful, evidence-based discourse accessible to everyone by transforming unstructured online arguments into organized discussions that promote understanding, critical thinking, empathy, and informed decision-making.

---

# 4. Why Discora Exists

Online discussions have become increasingly fragmented, emotional, and difficult to navigate. Social media platforms are designed to maximize engagement, attention, and reactions, often encouraging quick judgments, outrage, tribalism, and confirmation bias rather than thoughtful understanding.

Important conversations become buried under noise, misinformation, personal attacks, emotionally driven arguments, and incomplete context.

As a result, discussions rarely lead to meaningful conclusions. People often choose sides based on familiarity, identity, personal experiences, social influence, or the opinions they are most exposed to, rather than a complete understanding of the topic.

Over time, this contributes to division, hostility, stereotypes, and a lack of empathy between individuals and communities. People frequently become more attached to positions while understanding opposing perspectives less.

Discora exists to provide a better alternative.

Discora is a structured discourse platform where discussions and debates are organized around claims, evidence, sources, reasoning, and respectful disagreement. Instead of rewarding popularity or conflict, Discora encourages transparency, critical thinking, evidence examination, and informed discussion.

The goal of Discora is not to determine winners, enforce ideologies, declare absolute truths, or tell people what they should believe.

The goal is to help people reach the most informed understanding possible based on available evidence, reasoning, experiences, and perspectives.

Users are encouraged to question assumptions, strengthen arguments, revise opinions when necessary, and better understand the beliefs, experiences, and reasoning of others.

Discora envisions a world where disagreement does not lead to hatred, where discussions build understanding instead of division, where people can respectfully challenge ideas without attacking individuals, and where better conclusions emerge through open dialogue, evidence, empathy, and intellectual honesty.

# 5. Core Principles

## Freedom of Thought

Users are free to hold, express, question, and challenge ideas.

Discora does not promote or enforce any political, religious, cultural, or ideological position.

---

## Respect for Individuals

Ideas, claims, evidence, and reasoning may be challenged.

Individuals should be treated with respect regardless of their beliefs, background, identity, or viewpoints.

Challenge ideas, not people.

---

## Evidence Over Popularity

The popularity of an opinion does not determine its validity.

Evidence, reasoning, and transparency should be prioritized over likes, trends, or group pressure.

---

## Transparency Over Authority

Claims, evidence, assumptions, reasoning, and sources should be visible and open to examination.

Users should understand how conclusions are formed.

---

## Understanding Before Agreement

The goal of discussion is understanding, not forced agreement.

People may continue to disagree while still gaining a better understanding of the topic and each other.

---

## Intellectual Humility

Everyone can be mistaken, uninformed, or incomplete in their understanding.

Revising one's views in light of stronger evidence or reasoning is a sign of growth rather than weakness.

---

## Human-Centered Discourse

Evidence and facts are essential, but human experiences, emotions, and lived realities are also important components of meaningful discussions.

---

## AI Assists, Humans Decide

AI may organize, summarize, classify, analyze, and verify information.

AI must not determine what users should believe or act as the final authority on truth, morality, politics, religion, or ideology.

---

## Pursuit of Better Understanding

Discora exists to help users reach more informed, evidence-supported, and transparent conclusions while remaining open to new information and perspectives.

# 7. Core Concepts

## Discussion

A Discussion is an open, structured environment where individuals can share ideas, opinions, experiences, questions, news, observations, problems, solutions, and perspectives.

The purpose of a discussion is exploration, understanding, learning, and knowledge exchange rather than competition or persuasion.

Discussions may lead to deeper understanding, new questions, consensus, or structured debates when a topic requires more rigorous examination.

---

## Debate

A Debate is a structured examination of a topic, claim, proposal, or question that contains significant disagreement, uncertainty, or competing perspectives.

Through evidence, reasoning, questioning, and respectful challenge, debates help participants and observers better understand complex issues, evaluate different viewpoints, and move toward more informed conclusions.

Debates in Discora are not designed to create winners and losers, but to improve understanding, clarify disagreements, identify areas of consensus, and encourage critical thinking.

---

## Claim

A Claim is a statement intentionally presented for examination, discussion, or debate.

Claims serve as focal points around which evidence, counterarguments, questions, clarifications, and sources can be organized.

A claim does not represent an established truth. It represents an idea, assertion, observation, proposal, prediction, or conclusion that participants consider important enough to evaluate and discuss.

Claim Types:

* Fact Claim
* Opinion
* Prediction
* Proposal
* Observation

---

## Evidence

Evidence is any information, observation, experience, source, data, record, or material presented to support, challenge, clarify, or provide context for a claim.

Evidence does not determine truth on its own. It helps participants evaluate claims by providing relevant information, reasoning, or perspective.

Evidence Categories:

* Scientific Evidence
* Statistical Evidence
* Documentary Evidence
* Visual Evidence
* Experiential Evidence
* Expert Evidence
* Historical Evidence
* Logical Evidence
* Ethical Reasoning
* Cultural Evidence

Evidence should be evaluated in context, with awareness of strengths, limitations, and relevance.

---

## Source

A Source is the origin of information used as evidence within a discussion or debate.

Sources may include:

* Research Papers
* Articles
* Reports
* Datasets
* Books
* Videos
* Images
* Official Documents
* Personal Accounts
* Other Information Sources

Discora encourages users to evaluate sources based on transparency, credibility, relevance, methodology, context, and limitations.

AI may assist by summarizing sources, identifying key information, highlighting limitations, and providing context.

---

## Question

A Question is a request for understanding, clarification, information, perspective, reasoning, evidence, or exploration.

Questions help guide discussions and debates by identifying uncertainties, assumptions, knowledge gaps, and areas requiring further examination.

Question Types:

* Information Questions
* Clarification Questions
* Perspective Questions
* Evidence Questions
* Directional Questions
* Reflective Questions

Discora values meaningful questions as much as meaningful answers.

---

## Consensus

Consensus is a collection of shared understandings, conclusions, observations, or agreements that emerge through discussion, debate, evidence, and reasoning.

Consensus does not represent absolute truth, majority opinion, or forced agreement.

Instead, it reflects the most supported and broadly accepted understandings that participants were able to establish based on available evidence and perspectives at a given point in time.

Consensus remains open to revision as new evidence becomes available.

---

## Open Questions

Open Questions are important unresolved questions that emerge during discussions and debates.

They represent areas where sufficient understanding, evidence, agreement, or clarification has not yet been reached.

Open Questions help identify what remains uncertain, disputed, unexplored, or worthy of future investigation.

---

## Open Discussion

Open Discussion is the continuing state of a topic after a discussion or debate has reached its current conclusions, consensus points, and open questions.

It remains open to new evidence, perspectives, experiences, arguments, and developments that may challenge, refine, expand, or improve the current understanding of the topic.

Open Discussion recognizes that understanding is an evolving process rather than a final destination.

# 8. User Roles

## Participant

Participants may:

* Create Discussions
* Create Debates
* Create Claims
* Submit Evidence
* Add Sources
* Ask Questions
* Participate in Consensus Building

---

## Neutral Participant

A Neutral Participant is not required to support either side of a debate.

They may:

* Remain undecided
* Support aspects of multiple viewpoints
* Ask questions
* Provide context
* Share experiences
* Identify common ground
* Highlight strengths and weaknesses across positions

---

## Observer

Observers primarily consume information.

They may:

* Read Discussions
* Read Debates
* Explore Claims
* Review Evidence
* Learn from Discussions

---

## AI Moderator

AI Moderators provide soft moderation.

Responsibilities include:

* Spam Detection
* Harassment Detection
* Threat Detection
* Initial Content Review
* Source Analysis
* Information Organization

AI may flag or temporarily hide content pending review but may not make final moderation decisions.

---

## Human Moderator

Human Moderators perform hard moderation.

Responsibilities include:

* Content Review
* Appeals
* Rule Enforcement
* Final Moderation Decisions

Permanent moderation actions must be performed by humans.

---

# 9. Participation Rules

* Any user may create discussions.
* Any user may create debates.
* Debates may be created directly.
* Discussions may evolve into debates.
* Anonymous participation is supported.
* Similar discussions and debates should be suggested before creating new ones.
* Users may choose to join existing discussions or create new ones.
* Discussion creators do not have authority over other participants.
* Discussion creators cannot remove content posted by others.
* Ideas should be evaluated based on evidence and reasoning rather than status, influence, or popularity.

---

# 10. Moderation Philosophy

Discora moderates behavior, not viewpoints.

Users may challenge ideas, ideologies, beliefs, policies, and perspectives.

Discora does not moderate content simply because it is controversial or unpopular.

Moderation focuses on:

* Harassment
* Threats
* Doxxing
* Illegal Content
* Spam
* Repeated Abuse

AI may temporarily hide content that appears to violate platform rules.

Hidden content should remain visible as:

"Message Hidden Pending Review"

Permanent moderation decisions must be made by human moderators.

# 11. Topic System

Topics are organizational structures designed to help users discover related discussions and debates.

Topics do not restrict discussion.

Topics may exist as:

* Broad Topics
* Subtopics
* Emerging Topics

Users may create independent discussions that are not attached to any topic.

If a subject gains sufficient activity, it may become a recognized topic.

Examples:

Artificial Intelligence
→ AI Regulation
→ AI Safety
→ AI in Education

Education
→ Examinations
→ Online Learning
→ Higher Education

# 13. Knowledge Lifecycle

Discora views understanding as an evolving process.

Typical Lifecycle:

Discussion
→ Claims
→ Evidence
→ Sources
→ Consensus
→ Open Questions
→ Open Discussion
→ New Evidence
→ Updated Understanding

Alternative Lifecycle:

Discussion
→ Debate Request
→ Debate
→ Consensus
→ Open Discussion

Direct Lifecycle:

Debate Creation
→ Claims
→ Evidence
→ Consensus
→ Open Discussion

Consensus is not considered a final destination.

Topics remain open to revision as new information becomes available.

# 14. AI Systems & Principles

## AI Philosophy

Discora uses Artificial Intelligence as an assistant for organization, analysis, and understanding.

AI exists to help users navigate information more effectively, not to determine what users should believe.

The final responsibility for forming conclusions always belongs to humans.

---

## AI May

* Summarize discussions
* Summarize debates
* Analyze sources
* Extract evidence
* Organize claims
* Suggest consensus points
* Identify open questions
* Detect duplicate discussions
* Highlight contradictions
* Highlight supporting evidence
* Explain source limitations
* Provide context
* Assist moderation
* Detect spam
* Detect harassment
* Detect threats

---

## AI May Not

* Determine truth
* Decide ideology
* Decide morality
* Decide political correctness
* Decide religious correctness
* Decide debate winners
* Override human moderation
* Remove content permanently
* Force conclusions

---

## Source Intelligence System

One of Discora's core features is Source Intelligence.

Sources are not treated as simple links.

AI may:

* Read sources
* Extract metadata
* Generate summaries
* Identify authors
* Identify publication dates
* Identify source types
* Highlight limitations
* Extract evidence
* Connect sources to claims

Users should be able to understand a source without needing to fully read it first.

---

## Evidence Intelligence

AI may help:

* Organize evidence
* Group similar evidence
* Identify supporting evidence
* Identify contradicting evidence
* Highlight missing evidence

AI should never determine whether evidence proves a claim.

AI should only help users evaluate available information.

---

## Consensus Assistance

AI may suggest:

* Potential consensus points
* Areas of agreement
* Areas of disagreement
* Open questions

Consensus suggestions must always remain reviewable by users.

---

## Moderation Assistance

AI acts as a first layer of moderation.

AI may:

* Flag content
* Temporarily hide content
* Request review

AI may not make permanent moderation decisions.

---

# 15. Technical Constraints

## Cost Philosophy

Discora should be buildable, deployable, and maintainable with zero mandatory operating costs during development and MVP stages.

Paid services should remain optional.

---

## AI Requirements

The platform should support:

* Local AI Models
* Self-hosted AI Models
* Open Source AI Models

The platform should not depend on proprietary paid AI APIs for core functionality.

---

## Technology Principles

The system should be:

* Modular
* Scalable
* Extensible
* Maintainable
* AI-Ready

Future AI systems should be attachable without redesigning the platform.

---

## Architecture Principles

Discora should separate:

* Discussions
* Debates
* Claims
* Evidence
* Sources
* Consensus
* Users

into independent modules.

This allows future expansion without major architectural changes.

---

## Data Principles

All important information should remain structured.

Discora should avoid storing critical knowledge solely within comment chains.

Claims, evidence, sources, consensus points, and open questions should exist as first-class entities.

---

## Transparency Principles

Users should be able to understand:

* Why content was flagged
* Why content was hidden
* Where evidence came from
* How consensus was formed

Discora should avoid hidden decision-making wherever possible.
